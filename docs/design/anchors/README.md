# Anchors: Shaun's own ratings of real first screens (desktop, 1440 wide). They are the measuring stick for every page and every critic round.
He looked at six sites blind. One passes. Five do not (two of those were built by our own system and were called garbage).

PASS: `pass-01-daflores.jpg` (daFlores.com). Why it passes:
- One bright, full-width, professional photo with a real emotion in it (a person happy with the product), not a cut-out on a flat colour.
- One huge, elegant headline across the photo (about a quarter of the screen height), few words.
- One clear task directly under it (a single card: where do you want to send flowers, one orange button).
- Clean, light, airy. A lot of empty space. Small, quiet navigation. Nothing competes with the photo and the headline.

FAIL (all of these): `fail-01-elracimo.jpg`, `fail-02-laorquidea.jpg`, `fail-03-lafloreria.jpg`, `fail-04-our-nuit.jpg`, `fail-05-our-florist.jpg`.
What they have in common, and what our own two pages did wrong: dark or muddy colours, dense lists, tables and price rows competing for attention, small headline, photos that are dim, blurry, cropped or too close, no single emotional image, no single task. Information first, feeling last.

How to use this: a first screen must read as clearly better than every fail anchor and as strong as the pass anchor on four things: photo quality and brightness, headline scale, one clear task, space. If a business's own photos cannot reach that, use a labelled professional stand-in photo (CONTENT.md, "Photo verdict") so the owner sees what the site could be. Prices, menus and lists come below the first screen, never instead of it. Shaun adds new ratings here over time.
