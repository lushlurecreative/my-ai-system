# Design rules (master-owned; refreshed by the installer; read when a task is Type: DESIGN)

## Design work (how a page, site or screen looks)

Shaun wants work that looks designed for one business, at today's quality, never a template and never a copy of the last site. Text rules did not hold, so a hook now checks the session's own record. Anything that decides how a customer site looks is a task with `Type: DESIGN` (a small change to a page that already exists, such as wording, colour, spacing or a bug, stays a BUILD card with the line `Design edit: small`) and three extra card lines: `Design: <short-name>`, `Trade: <the business's trade>`, `Site: <path to the page>`, plus a `Rulings applied:` list (every recorded decision in docs/RULINGS.md that touches this design, each with how you honor it). A DESIGN task cannot be set to DONE, and you cannot end by claiming done, until the record shows all of this:

1. **Material.** `docs/design/<name>/CONTENT.md` lists the business's own photographs (files that exist), the number of real verified items, and any labelled designed assets. Below the minimum (system.json: designMinPhotos, designMinItems) you do not build, unless you use honest stand-ins (below): otherwise record the business as waiting for more material, tell Shaun in plain words which business and what is missing. Never invent products, prices, phone numbers or reviews.
1x. **Know when to call it quits (honest stand-ins).** After the investigation, judge the business's own photos and videos for real: look at every one and grade it. If they are unusable (blurry, dark, mostly close-ups of nothing, ugly, too few), do not use them just because they exist. Use labelled sample photos of the right trade instead, so the owner sees what their site could look like. In `CONTENT.md` write `Photo verdict: own | mixed | stand-in`, `Verdict reason:` (what you looked at, how many, why it fails), `Stand-in photos:` (files that exist) and/or `Stand-in items:`, and a `Replace later:` list with one line per stand-in or missing fact (each photo slot, a phone number or price you could not find, what the business should send). The page itself must say it visibly, for example a badge on each sample photo: "Foto de ejemplo - aqui ira tu foto". A fact you could not find is shown as an honest placeholder ("Tu telefono aqui"), never invented, and listed under `Replace later:`. Sample material is never shown as if it were the business's own, and good own material is never swapped for samples. When Shaun himself directs a build for a thin-material business (for example to compare it with an earlier page), add the line `Material waived: <his words and the date>` to the card; the page is then labelled a thin-material test.
1y. **Photos are measured, not described.** `CONTENT.md` names the first-screen image on a `Hero:` line. Every listed photo is run through `scripts/photo-check.py` (needs `python3` with Pillow and numpy): blurry, dark or tiny photos cannot be listed as usable, and the hero must clear a stricter bar (bright, sharp, at least 900 pixels on the short side). Photos that show people are allowed: a business's own good-quality photos belong to the business, and it is not our job to judge whether it shows a face; never remove or hold back a good photo because of who is in it. The check cannot tell a boring close-up (bread-crumb texture) from a good photo: that is for the anchors (item 2a), the independent reviewer and Shaun. Set `designPhotoCheck: false` in `.claude/system.json` only if Shaun says so.
1z. **No commentary on the site.** A page carries the business's own voice and facts, never notes about where they came from: no "on her Instagram (date)", no "according to", no source lines, dates in brackets, photo credits or research remarks, and no guesses about the owner (gender, family, history) that the research did not state. Copy is written as the business's own words or plain facts. The only allowed label on a page is the visible sample-photo badge (item 1x). Stand-in photos must come from sources that need no credit line (public-domain or no-attribution licences such as CC0, Unsplash or Pexels licences); never use a photo whose licence demands a credit on the page. The design gate scans the visible page text and refuses commentary.
1w. **Every photo once.** The same photo is never used twice on a page, and never on two different businesses' sites. Stand-in photos are drawn so that no two businesses share one; the design gate hashes the page's images and refuses a repeat on the page or one already used on another site in `designSitesDirs`.
1a. **Research is an investigation, not a checklist.** Treat every identifier you have (name, phone, handle, address, card) as the start of a lead queue, and keep working it until it is empty:
   - **Every thing found produces leads:** links, handles, phone numbers, addresses, names, hashtags, logos, document and catalog links. Each lead is followed, one after another, including the leads inside the pages you followed. A link-in-bio page, a catalog on a shared drive, a menu, a social page, a map pin: each gets its own line in `RESEARCH.md` (`- <type> | <link or none> | read, not-found or skipped | <evidence file path, the search you ran, or the reason>`), and a hook refuses the design while a found link has none. Types: website, instagram, facebook, tiktok, youtube, google-maps, delivery-marketplace, directory, link-in-bio, catalog-document, plus any others you find.
   - **Read everything, in every format:** posts and captions, videos (save the clips), documents and catalogs (open them page by page, save each page as an image), menus, price lists, reviews, tagged posts, the Google profile and its photos, the storefront and sign. Photos are taken at the best resolution available.
   - **Check the other side too:** third-party pages, directories, reviews and press about the business, not only what it publishes itself.
   - **Write the dossier** (`DOSSIER.md`, sections Identity, Brand, Offerings, Proof, Place and contact, Channels, Media, Gaps). Every section holds what was found or "none found: <how you searched>". Media has a count line (`Photos: n  Videos: n  Documents: n`). Gaps lists what is missing and what you tried.
   - **Test whether you are done:** run a second search from a different angle (by phone number, by handle, by tagline, by a photo) and write a `Saturation:` line with how many new leads it found. New leads mean the queue is not empty: follow them and test again.
   - Save everything in the project's real records, never only in a temporary folder. The business's own video and social channels come first. A task where research does not apply: `Research waived: <why>`.
1b. **The business's own identity first.** `docs/design/<name>/BRAND.md` records its real logo file, at least two brand colours as hex codes, where each came from (its logo, business card, sign, posts, website), and how the page uses them. Read the card, logo and storefront for colours and type, and trace a small or low-resolution logo into a clean vector so it is sharp at any size. The page must use at least two of those colours and the logo. The identity is where the design starts, never a coat of paint at the end. A business with truly no brand material: put `Brand waived: <why>` on the card.
1c. **Lead with the strongest medium.** Judge the business's photos and videos honestly. When its videos are stronger than its photos, the site is video-led: a looping video hero, video sections, stills only as poster frames. Never build around its weakest material.
2. **Learn from the best, avoid the local weak.** `REFERENCES.md` lists at least six of the BEST sites of the business's own trade anywhere in the world (rows `- same | <url> | - | <what great sites do here> | <what you take>`; up to two `neighbor` rows from a close trade), each really opened in this session with a screenshot. Never borrow from the customer's local competitors: they are usually weak. List at least two of them as what NOT to do (`- avoid | <url> | local | <what is weak about it> | <what we do instead>`), also really opened. The gate refuses a local competitor listed as something to borrow from. **Open each reference site once per trade, not once per business:** keep a shared library at `docs/design/library/<trade>/LIBRARY.md` with rows `- <url> | <screenshot file in that folder> | <YYYY-MM-DD> | <what to learn>`; a reference whose row has a real screenshot (at least 600 pixels wide) dated within 90 days (`designLibraryDays`) counts as opened without visiting it again. Only the local competitors listed as `avoid` are opened per business. Keep a pool of more than six per trade and let each business draw a different six, so pages do not converge. Judge the best sites by Shaun's anchors (item 2a), not by what is nearby.
2a. **Anchors are Shaun's own ratings.** `docs/design/anchors/` holds real first screens he rated: one that passes and several that do not (two built by this system), with `README.md` saying why. Every critic round opens them and reports an `ANCHORS:` line; a first screen that resembles a fail anchor, or does not reach the pass anchor on photo quality and brightness, headline scale, one clear task and space, fails. Information (prices, menus, lists) goes below the first screen, never instead of it. When Shaun rates more pages, add them there. Never judge a page's quality by your own eye alone, and never tell Shaun a page is good or ready: report only what it meets or misses against the anchors and the rules, and let the independent review and Shaun decide.
2e. **Only a quality FAIL costs a designer round.** The expensive designer stage runs once. A mechanical refusal (a missing brand colour, a picture count, renders not opened, commentary, a duplicate photo, a link format) is repaired and re-checked in seconds, in a loop, without rerunning the designer. Only the critic's FAIL on quality sends the page back to a designer round, and then to fix that critic's specific findings. Run the cheap deterministic checks before the designer stage as well, so the designer is never started on input that is already known to be refused.
2d. **Every failed check gets one of three outcomes: repair, skip or ask. Decide which, on purpose.** Code that builds pages or messages automatically keeps a registry of known failures and what to do with each. (1) **Repair** when the failure is in the work product and a repair exists: a dead or wrong WhatsApp link, a missing map or call or Instagram link, a missing trade tool, a one-colour logo, a blurry or dark photo, a headline over the product. Apply it, run the check again, and repeat while a pass makes progress. (2) **Skip** when the failure is about the business or the input, and building would be wrong or wasted: no readable listing or no real material even for honest stand-ins, the business already has a good site, it is not a business we should touch. We never prove who owns anything; we research. A found page or account is used only when it plainly matches the business (name and city, a phone number, or a link from a page already known to be theirs); if unsure, leave that source out and note it in the dossier. A business is skipped only when nothing real is left to build from. Skip early and cheaply, before expensive work, record the reason in one plain line, and move on. Skipping is a correct result, never an error. (3) **Ask** when one specific input is missing that only Shaun or the business can give (for example no WhatsApp number exists anywhere): say in plain words exactly what is needed, and meanwhile ship nothing that depends on it. Whatever the outcome, a button that cannot be proven to work is never shipped: it becomes a different working button (call, Instagram) or a clearly labelled replace-later placeholder.
2c. **Show Shaun the real page, not only pictures.** Every report about a page, a site or a screen starts with a working address he can click and use in his own browser: a local preview address with the preview server left running, or a published preview when publishing is allowed. Screenshots are extra, never the only thing, and never one tall image. State the address in plain text, say whether it works on his computer or needs the server running, and confirm you opened it yourself first. A page he cannot open is not shown.
2b. **Optional: start from a gallery design's real code.** Not required (starting from a gallery template produced the worst pages so far); use it only when one clearly fits the business. The galleries listed in `docs/design/SOURCES.md` (and the project's design catalog) hold thousands of curated, voted, top-rated designs, many with their real code readable or copyable. Pick the best fit for this business's mood and trade, open it, fetch its real source (HTML, CSS, JS; use the browser to reach a "copy code" button), save the files in `docs/design/<name>/BASE/` with `BASE/SOURCE.md` (`url: <link>`), and put `Base: <link>` on the card. Then adapt it: keep its structure, class names, motion and craft where they work, and replace the content, colours, photographs and language for this business. If you do, the gate (when the project turns it on with `designBaseRequired: true`) checks the page was really adapted from it and the base is used once. For craft, the base may be from any trade (the best-built sites are not all bakeries); the same-trade references above are for content and what customers expect.
3. **Design skills actually run.** At least three different ones from the design library (direction, layout, type and colour, polish, critique), run with the Skill tool on this design. If the library cannot be reached, stop and tell Shaun; never substitute your own judgment.
4. **A concept for this business** in `CONCEPT.md`: the idea drawn from its own world, the layout, type, colour and one signature moment; what you borrowed from each reference and how you made it yours; a `Different from:` section naming at least three earlier sites; and five style lines `Hue:`, `Fonts:`, `Hero:`, `Density:`, `Order:`. Three of the five must differ from every earlier concept. The page's structure must not repeat an earlier site.
5. **Renders you looked at.** Full-page phone and desktop PNGs in `shots/`, taken with the hidden browser. After your last change, open both yourself and look. Then film the scroll: `node ~/my-ai-system/scripts/filmstrip.mjs <page> docs/design/<name>/shots` gives frames at every step of the scroll (desktop and phone) and a recording, so the critic can judge motion. When the project turns the base rule on, a page also needs real motion and depth: at least two of scroll-driven or scroll-triggered animation, keyframe animation, smooth scrolling, sticky or layered scenes, 3D or shader graphics, view transitions. A still page is not acceptable, and it must stay light enough for a cheap Android phone (respect reduced motion).
6. **Two or more rounds with `design-critic`** (Agent tool, subagent_type design-critic). It looks at the images, compares the page with the closest site in `docs/design/BAR.md`, argues why Shaun would call it garbage, checks the references fit the trade and your rulings, and ends with a verdict. Fix what it names, change the page, run it again. The last verdict must be PASS and come after your last change.

**Automatic production code.** Hooks cannot watch code. A pipeline that makes customer-facing designs without a chat must itself call `node ~/my-ai-system/scripts/design-check.mjs . <name>` and refuse to publish unless it prints PROVEN. The pipeline writes `docs/design/<name>/EVIDENCE.json` from what it really did (the header of `scripts/design-check.mjs` lists the fields); a reference counts as opened only with a real screenshot on disk. A rule that only exists as text, or only in chat checks, is not enforced for code that makes the output.

Never: layout templates or presets for customer sites (shared code only for forms and buttons); a scrolling strip, a giant name with one photo as the whole first screen, empty picture boxes (see `docs/design/BANNED.md`); a separate headless Claude run with the skills switched off to make designs. The first three sites of any new trade go to Shaun together on one contact sheet before the rest. When a verdict says PASS, it means the process is complete; taste is still his call.
